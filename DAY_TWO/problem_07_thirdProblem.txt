## LEAN 4 PROOF EXPLANATION: PRINCIPLE OF EXPLOSION
Theorem: False → 1 = 2

CODE:
theorem false_implies_anything : False → 1 = 2 := by
  intro h
  exact False.elim h

STEP-BY-STEP BREAKDOWN:

1. "intro h"

* What it does: The goal is an implication `False → 1 = 2`. The `intro` tactic introduces the premise of the implication into our local context as a hypothesis. We name this hypothesis `h`.
* We now have a hypothesis `h : False` and our new goal is to prove `1 = 2`.

2. "exact False.elim h"

* What it does: The `exact` tactic solves a goal by providing a specific term of the correct type.
* "False.elim": This is the eliminator for the `False` type. Because the `False` type has no constructors (it is an empty type), if you somehow have a term of type `False`, you can use `False.elim` to construct a proof for *any* proposition. This is the logical principle of explosion (ex falso quodlibet).
* By applying `False.elim` to our contradictory hypothesis `h`, we can prove `1 = 2`, or any other statement, closing the goal.

KEY TAKEAWAYS / CORRECTIONS:

* The `False` type in Lean represents a logical contradiction. It has no constructors, meaning it is impossible to construct a term of type `False` in an empty context.
* When your context contains an impossible hypothesis (like `h : False`), you can prove anything using `False.elim`.
* Implication proofs (`A → B`) typically start with `intro`, assuming the premise (`A`) to prove the conclusion (`B`).
