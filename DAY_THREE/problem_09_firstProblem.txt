Title: Double Negation Elimination (not_not)

Problem: Prove that `¬¬P → P` for any proposition `P`. This is the principle of double negation elimination, a core tenet of classical logic.

Step-by-step Logic:
1. The goal is an implication, so we introduce the hypothesis `h : ¬¬P`.
2. We use the law of excluded middle (`by_cases`) on `P` to branch the proof into two possible scenarios: `P` is true, or `P` is false (`¬P`).
3. If `P` is true (`hP : P`), the goal is trivially solved since we already have a proof of `P`.
4. If `P` is false (`hP : ¬P`), we have both `¬P` and our original assumption `¬¬P` (which is equivalent to `¬P → False`). By applying `h` to `hP`, we derive a contradiction (`False`).
5. Since anything follows from a contradiction (the principle of explosion), we can use `False.elim` to prove `P`.

Tactics Used:
- `intro`: Introduces a hypothesis into the local context.
- `by_cases`: Branches the proof into two cases based on the truth value of a proposition, applying classical logic.
- `exact`: Closes a goal if the provided proof term exactly matches the goal's expected type.
- `False.elim`: Deduces any proposition from a contradiction (`False`).
